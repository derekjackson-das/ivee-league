Frequently asked questions

If yours isn't here, send it through the inquiry form and I'll answer within two business days.

How far ahead should we book?

Most couples book 9 to 14 months out. Peak Saturdays from May through October go first. Weekday and off-season dates are often open inside six months. The date checker gives you an instant answer for any date in the next two years.

What does it cost to hold a date?

A signed agreement and a 25 percent retainer hold the date. The balance is due two weeks before the wedding. (In this demo nothing is actually booked or charged.)

Do you work with a second photographer?

On request. A second photographer is worth it above about 120 guests, when both partners are getting ready in different places, or when the ceremony and reception run in parallel. It's a $600 add-on on Half Day and Full Day packages.

How many photographs will we get, and when?

There's no cap. A full day usually lands between 600 and 900 edited photographs. You'll see a preview of around 30 within a week and the full online gallery within six weeks (four for elopements).

Can we print the photographs ourselves?

Yes. Every package includes a print release for personal use. The gallery also offers prints and albums from a lab I trust, but you're never required to use it.

What happens if it rains?

We shoot anyway. Overcast light is kind to portraits, and a good rain photograph beats a nervous dry one. I carry clear umbrellas, and we'll have a covered backup spot in the timeline for any outdoor portrait session.

Do you travel outside Massachusetts?

Gladly. The service area covers most of New England, with flat travel fees by distance. Farther than that is quoted per wedding, including lodging. The cost calculator shows the bands.

Will our photographs end up on this website?

Only with your written permission, asked after delivery, never as a condition of booking. (This demo uses licensed stock images and no real clients.)

Is the website accessible?

That's the goal: WCAG 2.2 Level AA on every page, tested with axe and by keyboard. The accessibility statement lists known issues and how to report one.
